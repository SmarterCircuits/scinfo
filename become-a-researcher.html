How it works

You're a tester, not a ghost writer.

Each project will be a little different, but the basic workflow goes like this...

Email me

If you're interested in becoming a boffin, email me and tell me about your skills and a little about you.

This all started because I was offered a product designed for a pet and I do not have any pets. The product was interesting and I realized that I could share a bit of the spoils of the channel and get a little help making content at the same time.-

Response

I will let you know if I'm not looking for testers or if for some reason it's not a good fit, that way you're not wondering.

If we move forward, I would need an address to send products to. These products would be coming from their manufacturer or their distrubtors directly. Some of these companies will not ship to P.O.Boxes, so that may limit some options, but I completely understand if you don't want to give out your address.

Match

If a product comes in that needs a specific type of equipment, environment or expertise that I do not have, I look for someone who is a good fit on my list of testers. Priority will be closest fit and least worked with first to try to keep distribution even.

Planning

We will discuss what needs to be learned, what can realistically be tested, and how. We'll go over any special privacy or logistics requirements as well as timelines, testing periods, and possible later follow-ups.

Testing and filming

When you receive the product, you'll need to film the opening of the product with or without yourself (in disguise, masked, or blocked). Then, proceed with product setup and testing, making notes and taking as much footage as is reasonable and according to the agreed on plan.

Report back

When the testing is done, even if there will be later follow ups, send the footage and notes to me via the agreed upon methods and then we'll have a follow up conversation where we can brainstorm the video approach. Once we figure out a presentation, you'll need to then take any b-roll or pickup shots we discuss and send those to me. You don't need to do any more than just wave unless you want to. I need hero shots and demonstration, but those can be faceless if you prefer.

Posting and publishing

Once I'm done editing the video, I'll upload it somewhere for you to view it before it ever goes out. If there's a good reason to make any changes, we will. Then, the video may go to members only for a time before being ultimately made public.

Equipment: You will need a relatively decent camera. Most modern smart phones will do just fine. Even most old smart phones will do just fine. If you have more than one camera, that's even better. You don't need professoinal lights, but having bright light is critical to quality footage. I can offer some direction here where needed.
Who this is for

You do not need to be a YouTuber.

What matters most is curiosity, reliability, usable camera work and the ability to follow a test plan while also noticing things nobody thought to put on the test plan.

You would probably be a good fit if you…

  • enjoy testing, measuring, tinkering or figuring out how things work;
  • can record steady, well-lit video with a phone or camera;
  • can follow basic shot requests and repeat a demonstration when needed;
  • are comfortable giving candid feedback, including when a product is disappointing;
  • can communicate clearly and follow through on an agreed project;
  • have an unusual environment, hobby, pet, profession or technical specialty that creates useful test cases.

You do not need…

  • a public social-media presence;
  • a professional camera;
  • a perfect studio;
  • to speak on camera;
  • to reveal your real name or face to the audience;
  • to pretend to like a product that does not work well.
The gimmick — and the privacy feature

Your public identity can stay hidden.

Part of the fun is that guest researchers can appear as deliberately anonymous characters. A mask, helmet, hood, face covering, silhouette, unusual framing or some other visual disguise can turn each researcher into a recognizable recurring presence without requiring them to become a public personality.

You may not need to speak at all. Gestures, demonstrations, measurements, captions and voice-over added later can do a lot of the storytelling. If voice is useful, we can decide project by project whether to use it, process it, replace it with narration, or simply keep the segment silent.

The goal is not secrecy for secrecy's sake. It is to make participation possible for people who would enjoy helping with the research but have no interest in putting their face, name or personal life on YouTube.

What good footage looks like

Useful beats cinematic.

A modern phone is usually enough. The priority is that viewers can clearly see what is being tested and understand the result.

Stable

Use a tripod, clamp, shelf or other support whenever possible. Slow intentional movement is fine; accidental wobble is not.

Visible

Give the subject enough light. Avoid filming important details against a blown-out window or in a dark corner.

Repeatable

Leave a few seconds before and after an action. If something important happens too quickly, do it again from a better angle.

FAQ

Things worth knowing up front.

Do I have to show my face?

No. In fact, the anonymous-researcher format is specifically designed so that you do not have to. We can plan shots around a disguise, silhouette, hands-only framing or another approach that works.

Do I have to talk on camera?

No. Many tests can be shown visually and explained later with narration or on-screen text.

Do I get to keep the product?

Sometimes that may make sense and sometimes it may not. Product ownership or return requirements will always be agreed before shipment.

Is this paid?

Some collaborations may justify compensation, expenses or other arrangements, while others may simply involve testing a product you are interested in. Any such terms would be discussed before the project begins.

Will my real opinion be used?

That is the point. The value of the program is independent observation. A test is not useful if the conclusion is predetermined.

What kinds of products might need guest researchers?

Potentially anything within the broader Smarter Circuits world: smart-home hardware, sensors, networking gear, tools, electronics, appliances, energy products, unusual household devices, accessibility products and other technology that needs a real-world environment to evaluate properly.

Interested?

Tell me what makes you a useful test subject.

The most useful introduction is not a résumé. Tell me what you have access to, what you know how to do, what kinds of things you enjoy testing, and any oddball circumstances that might make you the perfect person for a future product.

It also helps to mention what you can film with, whether you are comfortable appearing in disguise, and whether you would prefer completely silent / hands-only participation.

Suggested response:
“I’m interested in being a guest researcher. I have access to ______, I know a fair amount about ______, I can film with ______, and I’d be especially useful for testing ______.”
